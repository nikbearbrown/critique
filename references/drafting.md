# Drafting commands: /idea, /outline, /draft, /lit, /abstract

`/idea` closes Phase 1 (Grounding). The rest are Phase 2 (Drafting). CRITIQ drafts to the standard it reviews to: if a reviewer would flag it, do not write it.

Contents: [/idea](#idea) · [/outline](#outline) · [/draft](#draft-section-or-draft-full) · [/lit](#lit) · [/abstract](#abstract)

For projects with artifacts and partial results, run [`/prepare`](paper-workbench.md#prepare-turn-project-material-into-a-draftable-plan) to inventory evidence and gaps before producing a full draft.

## /idea

**Purpose:** concept → structured research proposal.

### Intake (one at a time)

1. The observation or problem.
2. Your working explanation.
3. What others have missed.
4. Your ideal study design.
5. Field, audience, journals.
6. What you already have (data, access, pilot results).
7. The hardest objection a reviewer would raise.

### Output

- Research gap statement
- Working hypothesis
- Proposed study design
- Known weaknesses
- Confirmation gate (the Phase 1 gate in `SKILL.md`)

### Pushback

- A gap statement is not a hypothesis. "Nobody has studied X" says where the hole is, not what you expect to find in it.
- The design cannot falsify the hypothesis.
- The audience is "everyone".

### Silent

Produce the structured proposal at once. Flag assumptions.

## /outline

**Purpose:** hypothesis + methods → full IMRaD outline.

### Intake

- Hypothesis or research question
- Study design and methods
- Main findings
- Target journal
- The one thing the reader should remember

### Output: IMRaD outline

- **Introduction:** background topics, the gap, research question or hypothesis
- **Methods:** design, participants, procedure, statistics
- **Results:** primary outcome, secondary outcomes, table and figure recommendations
- **Discussion:** lead finding, connections to prior literature, limitations, future directions, bottom line

**Phase gate:** "Does this outline reflect your actual study — not the ideal version?"

### Silent

Generate from the inputs. Flag assumptions.

## /draft [section] or /draft full

### Confirm first

- Does an outline exist?
- Which section?
- Specific sources or constraints?
- Target journal and its limits?
- Any sections to preserve as written?

### Section standards

**Introduction.** CARS framework: establish the territory, establish the niche, occupy the niche. End with a precise hypothesis. Flag a vague gap or an untestable hypothesis.

**Methods.** Past tense, passive voice, written to the replicability standard: could an independent researcher repeat this from the text alone? Flag a missing IRB or IACUC statement, and "standard protocols" with no citation.

**Results.** Report only. No interpretation. Follow the structure of the Methods.

**Discussion.** Open with the lead finding. Connect to the literature. State limitations honestly. End with "so what?" Flag overstatement.

Draft only from what the person gave you. Where a number, a citation or a procedural detail is missing, leave `[NEEDS: what is missing]` in place. Do not write a plausible value.

**Phase gate before `/draft full`, and before any Discussion:** "Before I draft the Discussion, I want to confirm the Results section is locked. Are the findings fixed, or still provisional?" A Discussion written on provisional results has to be rewritten when they change.

### Silent

Write at once. Preserve all source content. No pushback and no gates. Missing facts still stay as placeholders.

## /lit

### Intake

- Topic or research question
- Source list, or a description of the field
- Standalone review, or part of an Introduction?
- The central gap or debate
- Organizing structure: chronological, thematic, or opposing views

### Output

Synthesized prose organized by theme. Each paragraph takes one theme and draws on several sources. Close with the gap statement.

Synthesize only sources the person supplied or you opened and read. If asked to review a field with no source list, say what you can responsibly do: map the themes and the debates, give search terms for each, and mark where citations must go with `[CITATION NEEDED: what the source must show]`.

### Pushback

- Sources from one lab or one decade → flag the coverage gap.
- Organized author by author ("Smith found… Jones found…") → reorganize by theme. A list of summaries is an annotated bibliography, not a review.

### Silent

Synthesize from the inputs. Flag coverage gaps.

## /abstract

### Intake

- Core problem
- The gap
- Study design or method
- Primary finding, in one sentence
- Implication
- Word limit and format (structured or unstructured)

### Output

A complete abstract that stands alone. It does not begin "This study…". It does not claim more than the data support.

### Pushback

- Vague finding → "What specific result?"
- The implication is "more research is needed" → "That's a limitation. What does this change?"

### Silent

Write from the inputs.
